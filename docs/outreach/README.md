## Before Robin sends a draft

Replace the bracketed sender, entity, contact and project placeholders. Confirm each recipient through the organisation's current official contact channel; retailer addresses are deliberately left blank. The Trade Me addresses come from the project brief; its developer homepage currently directs different use cases to different teams, so check the route before sending. Do not include API keys, passwords or unpublished personal information.

Keep only a project URL Robin is ready to share. These letters describe a proposed pilot; they do not assert an audience size, live retailer integration, existing partnership or ability to deliver an unbuilt feature. Any agreed refresh rate, data removal deadline, attribution or monitoring requirement must be reviewed against the implementation before Robin accepts it. Pricing is an invitation to discuss a fee per click or a fixed monthly fee, with no committed budget or assumed commission.

Ascent and Baby Factory passed the limited screening in [the retailer audit](../crawl-allowlist.md), which found no express restriction in the policies read. That finding is not consent. Their letters ask for consent and do not treat silence as acceptance. Other retailers need permission covering the restrictions identified in that audit. Any reply must come from someone authorised to grant the relevant rights; a friendly acknowledgement alone is not a licence.

## Scope and records

The retailer drafts request product identity, price, currency, GST treatment, factual specifications, availability, source URL and update time. They exclude images, original descriptions, reviews and customer data. A factual product name or identifier is for identification; branding and logo use require separate agreement. The project would disclose paid relationships and keep its recommendation criteria separate from payment.

The Trade Me draft is narrower: no retained copies of listing data, no history database, source links, and transient explainable rankings, subject to written approval. Trade Me's [API terms](https://developer.trademe.co.nz/terms-and-conditions) require approval for the purpose, control reuse and copies, and assign derivative information rights to Trade Me unless agreed otherwise in writing. Its [developer homepage](https://developer.trademe.co.nz/) warns that it does not respond to certain price monitoring and buyer tool requests. The draft therefore asks whether an exception is available; it does not present this as an ordinary supported integration.

Robin should keep each sent version, reply, authorised entity, approved fields and uses, retention and refresh rules, attribution, derivative rights, commercial terms, expiry and withdrawal conditions in the project's permission record. A draft or unanswered request must never be entered as an approved source. The project brief calls for New Zealand legal review before launch.
